## Details

### Bielik 11B v3.1 — SpeakLeash / ACK Cyfronet AGH (Poland), ~Nov 10
- 11B dense model, built on Mistral 7B by depth up-scaling. Trained on about 500B tokens.
- **Good for:** European languages (up from 32 to 56), using tools by itself, and multi-step tasks. It's small enough to self-host.
- Comes with **Bielik 11B v3.1 DFlash**, a draft model for speculative decoding, which makes generation faster.

### Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 — Anthropic (USA), Nov 9
- Both are the same underlying model. Fable 5.2 is available to everyone and has stronger cyber and bio safeguards. Mythos 5.2 is only for vetted organizations through Anthropic's Cyber and Life Sciences programs.
- **Good for:** the hardest coding and technical work, and long agentic tasks. Anthropic says output is up to 30% faster than 5.1. Reported scores: 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 (Fable) and 89.6% on CVE Rediscovery (Mythos).
- Price: $8 / $40 per million input / output tokens. Fast mode costs twice as much.

### Muse Ember 1.0 — Meta (USA), Nov 9
- Meta's smallest Muse model. Weights are closed. Available through Muse Code and the Meta Model API (`muse-ember-1.0`).
- 1M-token context and up to 512K output tokens. Takes text, image, audio and video as input and returns text.
- **Good for:** cheap, high-volume agent subtasks (tool calls, coding subtasks, extraction, routing, parsing long documents), paired with a larger model that does the planning. Meta says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5's Intelligence Index score at about a third of the cost.
- Price: $0.10 / $0.50 per million input / output tokens.

### GPT-6 Cyber — OpenAI (USA), Nov 5
- A security-specialized version of GPT-6 Astra. Only approved **Daybreak Red** customers can use it, through the API and Codex.
- **Good for:** authorized penetration testing, finding vulnerabilities, and exploit development. OpenAI reports 40.3% vulnerability recall, against 32.0% for GPT-6 Astra, and one report gives 48.5% on ExploitGym. OpenAI rates its cyber risk as "Critical".

### Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash — Google (USA), Nov 5
- Generally available in the Gemini API and Google Cloud.
- **Good for:** video generation, now up to 15 seconds (was 10), multi-turn conversational editing, and following reference material more closely.
- Price: $0.07 per second at 720p, about 30% cheaper than 1.1. From Dec 3, calls to `gemini-omni-1.1-flash` will automatically go to 1.2.

### Hy4 — Tencent (China), Nov 4
- Full release, following the August preview. The preview was a 770B MoE model with 49B active parameters, more than 1M context, and Apache 2.0 open weights.
- **Good for:** coding, visual understanding, computer use, long-horizon tasks and deep research. It's free in WorkBuddy and CodeBuddy until Nov 15, with a usage limit.
- Weights and system card are promised "in the coming days". A cheaper **Hy4 Flash** is coming soon.

### Kimi K3.1 Pro (open weights) — Moonshot AI (China), weights Nov 4
- The API launched Oct 23, and weights were reported on Hugging Face on Nov 4. It's a 2.8T-parameter MoE model with about 104B active parameters and 1M context, under a restrictive Kimi license.
- **Good for:** one of the largest open models for general reasoning and coding. Moonshot says it removed cyberattack training data before releasing it openly.

### Gemini 4 Carbon — Google (USA), Oct 28
- Based on Gemini 4 Argon. Google calls it its strongest model, especially for cybersecurity and long tasks. Reportedly #1 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0, with a score of 59.
- **Good for:** agentic coding, computer use, knowledge work, science and math, and cyber defense.
- Reports disagree on how widely it's available. Some say the rollout is limited, and it was missing from the API docs as of Nov 1.
